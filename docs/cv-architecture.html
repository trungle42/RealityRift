Reality Rift CV: architecture

A Python service that watches the laptop webcam, finds up to two players, recognises a fixed list of body poses, says which of two colour planes each player stands in, and streams the result to the game frontend as JSON.

  • Python 3.12
  • ONNX Runtime (no PyTorch)
  • rtmlib: YOLOX + RTMPose
  • FastAPI · WebSocket
  • port 8001
  • 47 tests, no hardware needed

1. Overview

The CV service is its own process, separate from the FastAPI/Gemini backend. The game frontend opens one WebSocket to it and receives a JSON message for every processed camera frame: who is in view, their skeleton, the pose they are making, whether they are frozen, and which plane they stand in. The game decides what those facts mean (a pose challenge passes when pose.label equals the requested pose and pose.stable is true).

What a frame yields

OutputMeaningWhere it comes from
people[]Up to 2 tracked players, ids 1 and 2, with bounding box and 17 COCO keypointsmodels.py, tracker.py
poseOne of 6 fixed poses or none; stable once held 0.4 sposes.py, temporal.py
zone / zones[]Plane A (blue) or B (orange) each player stands in, and who is in each planezones.py
frozen, motionTrue after 1 s of stillness (for the game's freeze challenge)temporal.py

3. How a frame is processed

Pipeline.process(frame) is the only place the stages are wired together. Everything before it (camera) and after it (server, demo) treats it as a function from an image to a JSON-ready dict.

Why detection runs only every 6th frame

The detector finds people from scratch; it is the slowest stage. Between detections each tracked person's box is rebuilt from their own previous keypoints, which is enough for two people in a webcam view. A detection frame uses the detector's fresh box and re-anchors the track; if a person left, the next few detection cycles fail to match and the track is pruned.

4. Tracking: who is "Person 1"?

The game has two players and no need to recognise someone who walks away and returns, so identity is per-session only. PersonTracker keeps at most 2 tracks and hands out the lowest free positive integer as the id, so a two-player game only ever sees ids 1 and 2.

5. Pose recognition

The pose list is fixed and small, so classification is geometry on the keypoints rather than a trained model. It uses only the nose, shoulders, elbows and wrists because a laptop webcam rarely sees the hips. Every distance is divided by the shoulder width S and measured along the shoulder axis, so results do not depend on how far the player sits or on head tilt. "Left" and "right" mean the player's own left and right.

PoseRule (S = shoulder width; wrist needs confidence ≥ 0.3)
hands_on_headBoth wrists at or above nose level and within 0.8 S of the nose.
hands_upBoth wrists more than 0.2 S above the nose (and not on the head).
left_hand_up / right_hand_upExactly one wrist above that line; the other may be down or unseen.
t_poseBoth wrists within 0.6 S of the shoulder line and more than 0.8 S outward along it; any visible elbow within 0.5 S of the line.
arms_crossedEach wrist has crossed the body midline (by more than 0.15 S) and sits 0.3 to 1.5 S below the shoulders.
Status: these thresholds are first estimates, checked against synthetic skeletons only. They have not yet been tuned on real people posing. Tune them in cv_app/poses.py and add a regression skeleton to tests/skeletons.py for every change.

From a noisy label to a "stable" pose

Per-frame labels flicker. PoseSmoother keeps a 0.6 s time window, takes the label that holds at least 60 % of it, and calls the pose stable once that label has held for 0.4 s. It is time-based rather than frame-based so it behaves the same at 8 fps or 30 fps. The game should treat a pose as "made" only when stable is true.

Freeze detection

MotionTracker measures how much the visible keypoints spread around their own average over the last second, in shoulder-widths. Below 0.06 S for a full second the player is frozen. Whole-body translation counts as movement, which is what a "freeze" challenge wants. motion is null until there is enough history.

6. The two colour planes

The planes are two full-height vertical bands over the camera view, blue on the left and orange on the right, with a neutral gap between them. A player is "in" a plane according to the horizontal position of their shoulder midpoint (or the centre of their box when the shoulders are not both visible). The frontend draws the planes from the zones list in every message, and reads occupants to see who is in which.

7. Mirroring

Players expect a selfie view: step to your right and move right on screen. So the output coordinates are flipped (x becomes 1 − x). The flip happens after inference, never before: the pose model labels the shoulder and wrist keypoints anatomically, so feeding it a mirrored image would swap left and right.

8. Threads and data hand-off

Inference never runs on the web server's event loop. Two ideas keep latency low: every hand-off is a single "latest value" slot that the producer overwrites, and consumers wait for something newer than what they last saw. A slow WebSocket client skips frames; it never builds a backlog.

9. Program files

Dependencies run strictly downward: entry points use the pipeline, the pipeline uses the stages, the stages use small pure modules. Nothing below pipeline.py imports it back.

cv_app/config.py

67 lines · no dependencies

Every tunable in one Config dataclass: camera, model preset, device, tracking limits, smoothing and zone timings, mirror. Config.from_env() reads the CV_* overrides.

  • Config, from_env()
  • used by server, pipeline, tracker, demo

cv_app/capture.py

73 lines · OpenCV

Reads the webcam on its own thread and keeps only the newest frame, so a slow consumer never sees a backlog. Picks DirectShow on Windows and AVFoundation on macOS.

  • Camera.start() / read(last_seq) / stop()
  • read blocks until a newer frame arrives

cv_app/models.py

83 lines · rtmlib, onnxruntime

Wraps the person detector (YOLOX) and the 17-keypoint pose model (RTMPose). Chooses the device (CUDA, CoreML, else CPU), sets the ONNX thread count, warms up, and falls back to CPU if an accelerator fails.

  • resolve_device()
  • PoseEngine.detect(frame) → top 2 boxes by area
  • PoseEngine.estimate(frame, boxes) → keypoints, scores

cv_app/tracker.py

118 lines · numpy

Associates detections with tracks by greedy IoU, assigns the lowest free id (max 2), decides when the detector is due, and prunes lost or duplicate tracks. Each Track carries its own smoother, motion tracker and zone tracker.

  • PersonTracker: due_for_detection, match_detections, prune
  • Track, iou, keypoint_box

cv_app/poses.py

79 lines · numpy

Pure geometry: keypoints in, pose label and confidence out. Also holds the COCO keypoint names, the index constants and the fixed pose list.

  • classify(kps, scores, thr) → (label, conf)
  • POSES, KEYPOINT_NAMES, NONE

cv_app/temporal.py

79 lines · numpy

State over time. PoseSmoother turns per-frame labels into a stable one; MotionTracker decides when a person is frozen.

  • PoseSmoother.update(t, label, conf) → {label, raw, conf, stable, heldSec}
  • MotionTracker.update(...) → {motion, frozen, stillSec}

cv_app/zones.py

59 lines · no dependencies

Defines the two planes and tracks which one each person is in, with a debounce and sticky edges.

  • Zone, make_zones(gap)
  • ZoneTracker.update(t, x) → {id, label, heldSec}

cv_app/pipeline.py

110 lines

The orchestrator. process(frame) runs detect, track, pose, classify and the temporal stages, applies the mirror flip, and returns the JSON-ready payload including fps and latency.

  • Pipeline.process(frame, t=None)
  • accepts an injected engine (tests use a fake)

cv_app/overlay.py

73 lines · OpenCV

Draws the translucent planes (brighter and outlined when occupied), skeletons, bounding boxes and labels from a payload. Flips the frame first when the payload is mirrored so text stays readable.

  • draw(frame, payload, thr)
  • used by the demo window and the MJPEG stream

cv_app/server.py

164 lines · FastAPI

Runner is the background thread that owns the camera and pipeline and publishes the newest result. create_app() exposes it over a WebSocket and a few REST endpoints.

  • Runner.start / stop / snapshot / publish
  • create_app(runner, start); module-level app

tools/demo.py

58 lines

Runs the same pipeline without the server and shows an OpenCV window. --no-window --frames N gives a headless smoke test that prints fps and the last result.

  • python -m tools.demo

tests/

47 tests · no camera or models

skeletons.py builds a synthetic COCO-17 person for each pose. A FakeEngine in test_pipeline.py stands in for the detector and pose model. Tests cover poses, invariances, smoothing, freeze, tracking, zones, mirroring and the server.

  • test_poses · test_temporal · test_zones
  • test_pipeline · test_pipeline_extras · test_server

12. Decisions worth knowing

ONNX Runtime, no PyTorch

The dev machine is Windows on ARM64 where PyTorch and MediaPipe wheels are unreliable; the deployment target is an Apple-silicon MacBook, where the onnxruntime wheel includes CoreML. One requirements file serves both.

Rules instead of a trained classifier

The pose list is fixed and small. Rules need no training data and are easy to read and test. Switch to a small trained classifier only if the rules prove brittle on real players.

Upper body only

Hips are rarely visible to a laptop webcam, so every rule uses the nose, shoulders, elbows and wrists, and players must face the camera.

Per-session ids

The game does not need to recognise a returning player, so there is no re-identification. Ids 1 and 2 are reused when someone leaves.

Newest-wins streaming

Real-time feedback matters more than every frame. Overwritten slots keep latency flat regardless of client speed.

Hand gestures: tried and removed

Built on request, then removed. The RTMPose hand model cost 150 to 250 ms per hand on the dev CPU; MediaPipe's hand model ran in about 20 ms. Details and pitfalls are in cv/CLAUDE.md.

Gotcha: a stray uvicorn cv_app.server:app process keeps the webcam open. If the demo or tests run at about 1 fps and find nobody, check for it before suspecting the code.

13. What is verified

Verified

  • 47 automated tests pass (synthetic skeletons, fake engine, server)
  • Live webcam: detection, keypoints, tracking, freeze, plane assignment and overlay render
  • Server: WebSocket push, REST endpoints and MJPEG stream with the real camera and models
  • Throughput about 23 to 35 fps on the dev CPU (Snapdragon X, no GPU)

Not yet verified

  • Pose thresholds on real people posing (only synthetic skeletons so far)
  • Two real players in view at once
  • CoreML on the MacBook (device selection is implemented but untested)
  • Frontend integration with /ws/cv
